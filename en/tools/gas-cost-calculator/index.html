Gas money calculator: split fuel costs on a road trip

Enter the distance, your car's fuel economy and the price of fuel. We'll tell you how much fuel you'll use, what the drive costs with tolls, and how much each person chips in.

Everything runs in your browser: nothing is sent or stored.

How it's calculated

The math is simple, and worth knowing so you can double-check it:

  1. Fuel: the distance (doubled for a round trip) divided by your miles per gallon. With litres per 100 km, it's the distance times that number, divided by 100.
  2. Cost: fuel used times the price per gallon or litre, plus tolls and parking.
  3. Per person: the cost divided by the number of people riding in that car.

An example: a 200-mile drive to the lake, round trip, is 400 miles. A car that gets 30 mpg uses about 13.3 gallons. At $3.50 a gallon that's $46.67, plus $12 in tolls: $58.67. Split four ways, each person chips in about $14.67.

How to find your car's fuel economy

  • The trip computer, if your car has one, shows average consumption. Highway figures are usually better than city ones.
  • The full-tank method: fill up, reset the trip meter, and at the next full fill-up divide the miles driven by the gallons you added (or litres by km, times 100).
  • The manufacturer's rating is a useful reference, though real-world numbers tend to be a bit worse.

A loaded car, air conditioning and high speeds all push consumption up. To be safe, assume a little worse than your usual figure.

Should the driver pay the same as everyone else?

There's no single right answer, so agree on it before you leave. The most common options:

  • Everyone splits fuel equally, driver included. The simplest option.
  • The driver doesn't pay for fuel, as compensation for providing the car and the wear and tear.
  • Passengers add a little extra for wear and tear, for example 10 to 20% on top of fuel.

For the second option, put only the passengers in "People in the car".

If you're taking more than one car

Run the numbers once per car. If both cars are heading to the same place for the same trip, the fairest approach is usually to add up all the fuel and split it across the whole group, so nobody pays more just for riding in the thirstier car.

On a longer trip, fuel is just one expense among many: groceries, meals, nights out, tickets. FAQ

How do I calculate gas money for a road trip?

Divide the total miles by your car's mpg to get gallons, multiply by the price per gallon, add tolls and parking, and divide by the number of people. The calculator above does exactly that.

Does it work with litres and kilometres?

Yes. Choose "litres per 100 km" or "km per litre" and the calculator asks for the distance in km and the price per litre.

Should I count the drive back?

If everyone is coming back in the same car, yes: keep "Round trip" checked. If some people are only riding one way, run the calculation for each leg separately.
