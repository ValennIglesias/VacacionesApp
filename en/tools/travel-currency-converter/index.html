Which rate will you actually pay?

The mid-market rate is a reference: it's what banks trade at and what search engines show. What you actually pay depends on how you pay:

  • By card, your bank converts at its own rate and may add a foreign transaction fee. Cards without that fee get you close to the mid-market rate.
  • With cash exchanged before the trip, the real cost is whatever you paid for that cash.
  • At exchange offices abroad, there's a spread between buy and sell rates, so you get somewhat less than the mid-market rate. Airport counters are usually the worst.

One rule that always holds: if the card terminal asks whether to pay in your home currency or the local one, choose the local currency. The merchant's conversion is almost always worse than your bank's. Our guide to paying abroad with cards and cash goes into the details.

FAQ

Where do the rates come from?

The mid-market rate comes from a public exchange-rate service and is refreshed once a day. If you convert to or from Argentine pesos, we also show Argentina's dollar rates (official, card, MEP and blue), refreshed every hour.

Why did my bank charge a different amount?

Banks apply their own rate from the day they process the charge, which may not be the day you paid, and some add fees. The difference is usually small, but it's there.

Is it better to pay by card or in cash?

It depends on the country. In many places a no-fee card is the cheapest and safest option; in others, some shops give a discount for cash. Carry a little local cash for small purchases either way.
