Split expenses calculator: who owes whom

Enter what each person paid and we'll tell you who pays whom, with the fewest possible transfers. No account needed, and ready to paste into the group chat.

NameAmount paidShares

Shares: 1 is the default, 2 counts double (a couple, say), 0.5 is half and 0 means they're left out.

Everything runs in your browser: names and amounts are never sent or stored.

How to use it

  1. Type each person's name and how much they paid in total. If someone paid nothing, leave the amount empty.
  2. If not everyone should pay the same, change their shares. Everyone starts with 1 share.
  3. The calculator adds everything up, splits it by shares and works out each person's balance: what they paid minus what they owe.
  4. From those balances it builds the list of transfers: who pays whom, and how much.
  5. Tap "Copy result" and paste it in your group chat. A link to this page goes at the end, so anyone can double-check the math.

When people shouldn't pay the same: shares

An equal split isn't always fair. The "Shares" column lets you adjust what each person owes without doing the math by hand:

  • A couple or a family on one row: enter 2 (or 3, or 4). They pay like two people and get a single transfer.
  • A birthday dinner or a gift: give the birthday person 0. If they paid for anything, they get it all back.
  • Someone who was there half the time: enter 0.5, for example a friend who arrived on Saturday instead of Friday.
  • A kid, or someone who skipped the drinks: a smaller share such as 0.5, if the group agrees.

Why "the fewest transfers"

If everyone paid back each person's share of each expense, four people could end up making up to twelve transfers, many of them going back and forth. The calculator only looks at each person's final balance and pairs whoever owes the most with whoever is owed the most, over and over. That way you never need more transfers than the number of people minus one. The guide on settling group debts with fewer transfers goes into detail.

When the app is a better fit

The calculator works when every expense is split the same way and you already know what each person paid. For a trip of several days it often falls short:

  • Each expense has different people in it: the dinner only three went to, or the tour two of you took.
  • There are lots of expenses a day and by the end nobody remembers who paid for what.
  • You pay in several currencies on a trip abroad.
  • You want to settle up halfway and keep adding expenses afterwards.

That's what %BRAND% is for: everyone logs what they pay as it happens, from their phone, and the app does this same math on its own. It's free and nobody needs an account to join.

FAQ

Is the data I enter saved?

No. The math runs in your browser and nothing is sent to any server. Close the page and it's gone.

Does it work with any currency?

Yes, as long as every amount is in the same currency. If some expenses were in another currency, convert them to one first; this guide explains how to do it fairly.

What if someone didn't take part?

Give them 0 shares: they pay nothing and, if they covered something, they get it all back. If they joined some expenses but not others, it's easier to log each expense separately in the app, where you choose who takes part in each one.

Can shares have decimals?

Yes: 0.5 is half a share and 1.5 is one and a half. It's handy for someone who stayed fewer days, or for splitting rent by the nights each person stayed.

What happens to cents that don't divide evenly?

They go one by one to the first people on the list who take part, so the total adds up exactly and nothing is lost or left over.
