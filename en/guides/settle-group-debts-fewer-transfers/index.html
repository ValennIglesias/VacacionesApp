How to settle group debts with the fewest transfers

When six people share expenses for a week, the crossed debts look like a maze. A simple method resolves them in a handful of payments.

Updated October 18, 2026 · 2 min read

The crossed-debt problem

If every expense triggers everyone paying back their share, a week-long trip can generate dozens of small transfers, many in opposite directions. You don't need to make all of them. What matters is how much each person paid in total and how much their total share was.

Step 1: each person's net balance

Balance = what you paid − your share

  • Positive: you're owed money.
  • Negative: you owe money.
  • All balances always add up to zero.

Step 2: match debtors and creditors

Take the person who owes the most and the person owed the most. The first pays the second the smaller of the two amounts. One of them reaches zero; repeat with the rest.

PersonPaidShareBalance
Ana500250+250
Ben200250−50
Cara300250+50
Dan0250−250
  1. Dan pays Ana 250. Both are even.
  2. Ben pays Cara 50. Done.

Two transfers instead of six or more.

How many transfers at most?

For a group of N people you never need more than N − 1 transfers, often fewer.

Details that matter

  • Rounding: splitting evenly can leave a few cents over. Assign them consistently so totals add up exactly.
  • Partial settlements: expenses already settled mid-trip shouldn't be counted again at the end.
  • Confirmation: the recipient confirms each payment so everyone knows what's pending.
In practice: you don't need to do this by hand. The app computes balances and proposes the fewest transfers, in the currency you choose.
