How to plan a group trip budget

A good budget isn't a guess. It separates what you already know from what you need to estimate, and leaves room for the unexpected.

Updated October 20, 2026 · 2 min read

1. Separate fixed and variable costs

Fixed costs are known before you leave: flights, lodging, car rental, insurance, pre-booked tours. Variable costs depend on each day: meals, local transport, nights out, shopping.

Add up the fixed costs and divide by the number of people. Estimate the variable ones per person, per day.

2. Estimate daily spending per person

Picture a typical day on the trip and estimate:

  • Food: breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks. Cooking a few meals at your rental lowers this number a lot.
  • Local transport: taxis, public transport, fuel, parking.
  • Activities: tickets, rentals, tips.
  • Going out: bars, cafés, treats.

Multiply that daily figure by the number of days. If you don't know the destination, look up supermarket and restaurant prices on travel forums to calibrate.

3. Add a buffer for surprises

Something always comes up: an unplanned tour, a late-night taxi, a pharmacy run. A buffer of 10 to 15 % on top of the total is usually enough for short trips. For long trips or destinations with volatile prices, add a little more.

4. Agree on a spending level

This is the delicate part. If one person wants a nice restaurant every night and another prefers to cook, the same budget won't work for both. Options that work:

  • Agree on a shared "base" level and let anything extra be paid individually.
  • Mix it up: some meals at the rental, some meals out.
  • Split each activity only among the people who join, instead of splitting everything among everyone.

5. Track spending during the trip

A budget only helps if you compare it with reality. Logging every expense in a shared app lets everyone see, at any moment, how much you've spent in total, by category and by day. If food spending is running well over plan halfway through, there's still time to adjust.

Expenses before the trip count too

Deposits, flights and tickets are often paid weeks in advance by one person. Log them as soon as they're paid so they're part of the final split and nobody has to remember them later.

Key: agree on the budget before, log expenses during, and glance at the summary halfway through. That way the trip doesn't end with surprises.
