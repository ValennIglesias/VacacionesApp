How to handle travel expenses in multiple currencies

Traveling abroad adds a layer: you pay in local currency, sometimes in dollars or euros, and you pay each other back in your home currency. Keeping it straight is easier than it looks.

Updated October 18, 2026 · 2 min read

Rule 1: log each expense in the currency you paid

Paid dinner in pesos? Log it in pesos. Paid a tour in dollars? Log it in dollars. Converting in your head on the spot introduces errors, and today's rate isn't next week's.

Ideally your tool uses the destination's currency by default and lets you log an expense in another currency when needed.

Rule 2: convert foreign-currency expenses at that day's rate

When an expense is paid in a currency other than the trip's, convert it at the rate of the day it was paid and lock that rate. That expense keeps its value even if the currency moves later.

Rule 3: agree on the currency you'll settle in

The currency you spend in and the currency you settle in can differ. A group from Spain traveling in Mexico spends pesos and probably settles in euros. The practical approach:

  1. Compute all balances in the trip's currency.
  2. When settling, convert only the final transfers into the currency you'll pay in.
  3. Agree on which exchange rate to use for that conversion.

Which exchange rate?

  • International market rate: published by exchange services and central banks. A good neutral reference.
  • Your card's rate: what your bank actually charged, usually including fees.
  • Exchange office rate: if you changed cash.

None is "the right one" for every group. What matters is agreeing before you settle.

Common mistakes

  • Adding different currencies together without converting.
  • Converting everything at the end with the last day's rate, even though expenses happened weeks apart.
  • Not agreeing on the settlement rate until debts already exist.
Summary: log in the currency you paid, lock the rate for each foreign-currency expense, compute balances in the trip currency, and convert only the final transfers.
