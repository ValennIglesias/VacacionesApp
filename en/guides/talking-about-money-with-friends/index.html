How to talk about money with friends (and ask to be paid back)

Talking about money with friends feels awkward. Not talking about it ends up feeling much worse.

Updated October 20, 2026 · 2 min read

Why it's so hard

Asking a friend for money can feel like distrust, and chasing a small debt can seem petty. So many people quietly absorb the cost, and over time that silence turns into resentment. The problem isn't the money: it's that nobody talked about it.

1. Talk before, not after

The best time to talk about money is before you spend it. Before a trip, a night out or a shared purchase, agree on:

  • How it will be split (evenly, everyone pays their own, only those who take part).
  • Who pays upfront and when they get paid back.
  • Which currency and exchange rate, if that applies.

Rules are much easier to agree on when nobody owes anything yet.

2. Use a shared record

When expenses are logged somewhere everyone can see, the conversation stops being "you owe me" and becomes "here's what's left". The record takes the personal edge off: it's not someone complaining, it's the numbers.

3. Ask plainly, with the details

If someone hasn't paid, a direct, friendly message works better than hints. For example:

"Hi! We've closed the trip accounts. Your share comes to 120, here are my payment details. Thanks!"

Specific, with the exact amount and how to pay. Making the payment easy is half the job.

4. Set a reasonable deadline

Settling up in the first few days after the trip, while everything is fresh, keeps debts from being forgotten. On long trips, settling halfway keeps anyone from building up a big debt.

5. If you disagree, go back to the rules

If someone feels they overpaid or that an expense isn't theirs, look at the record and the original agreement together. Often it's a simple mistake: an expense split among everyone that should have been split among a few. Fixing it is easy when it's written down.

6. Confirm payments

When the person receiving confirms each transfer, it closes the matter for both: nobody is left wondering whether it arrived.

A few phrases that help

  • Before: "Shall we split everything evenly and just log who pays what?"
  • During: "I paid for the groceries, I'll add it to the list."
  • After: "Accounts are closed, everyone can see their balance. Let's settle this week."
In short: agree before spending, keep a record everyone can see, ask clearly and kindly, and confirm payments. Friendships appreciate the clarity.
