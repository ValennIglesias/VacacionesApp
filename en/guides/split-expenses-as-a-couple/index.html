How to split expenses as a couple: methods and examples

There's no single right way for couples to split costs, only the one that works for both of you. What matters is choosing it deliberately and that both of you feel it's fair.

Updated October 18, 2026 · 2 min read

Method 1: 50/50

Every shared expense is split evenly. Simple and transparent. It works well when incomes are similar; when one partner earns much more, the other ends up spending a larger share of their income on shared costs.

Method 2: proportional to income

Each partner contributes according to what they earn.

IncomeSharePays of 1,000 rent
Partner A3,00060%600
Partner B2,00040%400

Fairer when incomes differ, but it requires talking openly about money.

Method 3: a joint pot

Both put a fixed amount (equal or proportional) into a shared account, and shared costs come out of it. No need to split each expense, but contributions need adjusting when costs change.

Method 4: by category

One covers rent, the other groceries and utilities. Practical, but category amounts drift over time and the split can become unbalanced without anyone noticing. Review it now and then.

Things worth deciding

  • Are trips and nights out split the same way as household costs?
  • What about gifts, each other's families or pets?
  • How do you handle individual purchases that benefit both?

Keeping a record helps more than you'd think

Even with full trust, logging shared expenses prevents the "I always pay" feeling. Note who paid what and settle once a month, or at the end of a trip.

Key: the best method is the one you both feel is fair. Start with one and change it if it doesn't work. Just make it explicit.
